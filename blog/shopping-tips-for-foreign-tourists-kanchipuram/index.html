Shopping Tips for Foreign Tourists Visiting Kanchipuram

Shopping in Kanchipuram as a foreign visitor comes with a few extra considerations — from understanding an unfamiliar product to practical logistics like packing and customs. Here's what to know.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 2 Feb 2026 · Updated 2 Feb 2026 · 7 min read

Understanding What You're Buying

If you're not familiar with sarees as a garment, take a little time to understand the basics before shopping — how a saree is worn, what the blouse piece is for, and why the border and pallu matter visually. Shop staff are generally happy to explain this to international visitors, and it makes the whole shopping experience more meaningful rather than just visually overwhelming.

Language and Communication

Larger showrooms catering to a broad customer base, including tourists, typically have staff comfortable communicating in English, though smaller local shops may rely more on Tamil. If language is a concern, a larger established showroom is generally the more comfortable starting point.

Quick Tip: A local guide or your hotel can help bridge any language gap and often knows which shops are most comfortable dealing with international visitors.

Payment and Currency

Larger showrooms typically accept major cards and sometimes international payment options, while smaller shops and street vendors are more likely to be cash-only. It's worth carrying a reasonable amount of local currency in cash, alongside a card, to cover both scenarios comfortably.

Packing and Carrying Sarees Home

Silk sarees fold compactly and are relatively light, making them easy to pack in checked luggage, but it's worth asking the shop for proper folding and packaging to avoid unnecessary creasing during a long flight home. If you're buying multiple sarees, consider packing them separately from heavier items to minimise pressure creases.

Customs and Duty Considerations

Textile purchases like sarees are common tourist buys, but rules and duty-free allowances vary by destination country, so it's worth checking your home country's customs guidelines for textile imports before making a very large purchase, particularly for bulk or high-value buys.

  • Start with a larger showroom if language or unfamiliarity with sarees is a concern
  • Carry both cash and card, since acceptance varies by shop size
  • Ask for proper folding/packaging to protect your purchase during travel
  • Check your home country's customs rules on textile imports for higher-value purchases
  • Keep your bill — useful both for customs purposes and for any future questions about the saree's material
Can international visitors get tax refunds on purchases in India?

Tax refund schemes for tourists vary and change over time, so it's best to check current rules through official government tourism or customs channels rather than assuming a specific policy applies.

Is bargaining expected as a foreign tourist?

Large showrooms generally have fixed pricing regardless of who's buying, so aggressive bargaining isn't typically productive there. Smaller shops may have slightly more flexibility, but this isn't specific to being a foreign visitor.
