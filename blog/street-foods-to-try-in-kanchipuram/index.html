Street Foods to Try in Kanchipuram

Between temple visits and silk shopping, Kanchipuram's street food stalls offer quick, satisfying snacks that give a genuine taste of local everyday food culture.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 29 Jan 2026 · Updated 29 Jan 2026 · 6 min read

Why Street Food Is Worth Trying

Street snacks in Kanchipuram, concentrated mainly around the main market and bus stand areas, offer a quick, inexpensive way to experience local food culture between sightseeing stops, without committing to a full sit-down meal.

What to Look For

  • Bajji — vegetables like plantain, potato or onion dipped in gram-flour batter and deep fried, a classic South Indian tea-time snack
  • Bonda — similar deep-fried snack, usually with a potato-based filling
  • Fresh fruit stalls — a refreshing, simple option between temple visits, especially in warmer months
  • Filter coffee from small local stalls — often just as good as what you'd get at a sit-down restaurant
  • Local sweet shops near the market — worth a look for regional sweets and snacks to take home
Quick Tip: Choose stalls with visible, steady customer turnover — busy stalls generally mean fresher, more frequently prepared food.

Food Safety Basics for Visitors

As with street food anywhere in India, visitors not used to local food and water conditions should take a few sensible precautions — stick to bottled or well-known filtered water, avoid raw, unwashed produce from unclear sources, and choose freshly cooked hot items over anything that's been sitting out.

Warning: If you have a sensitive stomach or are new to Indian street food generally, start with smaller portions from busy, reputable-looking stalls rather than diving in extensively on your first day.

Pairing Street Food With Your Itinerary

Street snacks work particularly well as a light bite between a morning temple visit and a full lunch, or as an afternoon pick-me-up during a long silk-shopping session, rather than as a full meal replacement.

Is street food in Kanchipuram generally vegetarian?

Yes, in keeping with the town's broader vegetarian food culture, the vast majority of street snacks available are vegetarian.

What's a good first street snack to try if I'm new to Indian street food?

Bajji is a good, approachable starting point — familiar in concept (a fried, battered snack) for most visitors, widely available, and easy to order even without knowing the local language well.
