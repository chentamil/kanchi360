Complete Tourist Guide to Kanchipuram: A Practical One-Day Itinerary

Kanchipuram is compact enough to cover its major highlights in a single well-planned day, but the town rewards those who don't rush it. Here's a realistic itinerary that balances temples, food and shopping.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 8 Jan 2026 · Updated 8 Jan 2026 · 9 min read

Getting Oriented

Kanchipuram sits roughly 75 km southwest of Chennai, making it an easy day trip by road, or a comfortable overnight stop if you plan to combine it with nearby Mahabalipuram or Vellore. The town is compact — most major temples and the silk shopping streets are within a reasonably short auto-rickshaw ride of each other, so a single day is enough to see the highlights without feeling rushed, provided you start early.

Morning: Temple Visits (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM)

Start early to beat both the heat and the crowds. Kanchipuram is home to several major temples, each with its own character — Ekambareswarar Temple, dedicated to Shiva, is known for its vast pillared halls and an ancient mango tree in its courtyard; Kailasanathar Temple, one of the oldest structures in the town, is prized for its Pallava-era stone carvings and is quieter than the more actively worshipped temples; and Varadharaja Perumal Temple, dedicated to Vishnu, is known for its ornate hall of carved pillars. Kamakshi Amman Temple, dedicated to the goddess Kamakshi, is another major stop and one of the most visited shrines in the town.

Quick Tip: Dress modestly for temple visits — shoulders and knees covered — and be prepared to remove footwear before entering temple premises, as is standard practice across South Indian temples.

Midday: Lunch (12:30 PM – 1:30 PM)

Kanchipuram, like most South Indian temple towns, has a strong vegetarian food culture, and a simple South Indian thali or meal on a banana leaf is the natural choice here. Look for busy, local, no-frills restaurants near the temple areas — a full local lunch, popularly called 'meals', typically includes rice, sambar, rasam, a couple of vegetable preparations and buttermilk, and is both filling and inexpensive.

Afternoon: Silk Shopping (2:00 PM – 5:00 PM)

After lunch, head to the silk shopping area around Gandhi Road and Thirukachi Nambi Street. Give yourself real time here — comparing sarees across a few shops, understanding zari types, and simply admiring the range takes longer than most first-time visitors expect. If you're buying for a wedding, plan for at least two to three hours.

Silk shopping in Kanchipuram is best approached unhurried

Evening: One More Temple or Local Market (5:30 PM Onward)

If energy allows, a visit to one more temple in the cooler evening light — many temples have distinct evening rituals and lighting that feel very different from a midday visit — or a slower walk through the local market streets makes a satisfying close to the day before heading back to Chennai or to your hotel for the night.

  • Start by 8:00 AM to make the most of cooler morning hours at the temples
  • Carry water and wear comfortable, easily removable footwear
  • Keep small cash handy — many small eateries and local shops don't accept cards
  • If travelling from Chennai for the day, plan your return before late evening given intercity road traffic
Is one day enough to see Kanchipuram properly?

One day is enough to see the major temples and do meaningful silk shopping, but if you want to see a working handloom, visit more than 3-4 temples in depth, or shop unhurried for a wedding, an overnight stay gives you a much more relaxed pace — see our two-day itinerary guide for that version.

Do I need a guide to visit the temples?

It's not mandatory, but a local guide adds real value at temples like Kailasanathar and Ekambareswarar, where the historical and architectural context is easy to miss without someone explaining it. Guides can typically be arranged near the temple entrances or through your hotel.
