Where to Buy Groom Wedding Dresses in and Around Kanchipuram

Groom shopping gets far less attention than bridal shopping, but traditional South Indian weddings still call for specific pieces — here's what to look for and how to coordinate with the bride's saree.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 13 Jan 2026 · Updated 13 Jan 2026 · 7 min read

Traditional vs Contemporary Groom Outfits

Many South Indian grooms wear a traditional silk veshti (dhoti) paired with an angavastram (a shoulder cloth) for the core wedding ceremony, often in silk with a zari border to match or complement the bride's saree. For the reception or other functions, it's increasingly common for grooms to switch to a sherwani, kurta-set, or contemporary suit, giving more flexibility than the bride typically has across functions.

Coordinating With the Bride

If the bride is wearing a traditional Kanchipuram silk saree for the Muhurtham, many couples choose a veshti-angavastram set in a complementary silk and zari tone rather than an identical match — a shared colour family (for instance, both in gold-bordered maroon and cream tones) usually photographs better than trying to match exactly.

Quick Tip: Bring a photo or swatch of the bride's saree when shopping for the groom's veshti and angavastram so the shop can help you find a genuinely complementary — not clashing — combination.

Buying vs Renting

Silk veshti-angavastram sets are generally more affordable than bridal sarees, so buying is common and the set is often reused for other family functions afterward. Sherwanis and more elaborate contemporary outfits for the reception, on the other hand, are frequently rented, especially if the groom isn't likely to wear that specific style again.

  • Traditional veshti-angavastram silk sets for the main ceremony — usually bought, not rented
  • Sherwani or contemporary suits for the reception — commonly rented unless it will be reused
  • Match zari tone and colour family with the bride's outfit rather than aiming for an identical match
  • Factor in accessories — a simple angavastram pin, footwear and any traditional jewellery like a chain
Do grooms need different outfits for each wedding function like brides do?

It's common but generally simpler than bridal shopping — a traditional silk veshti-angavastram for the ceremony and a separate outfit (often rented) for the reception covers most needs, without the same number of distinct looks a bride typically plans for.

Is it acceptable for the groom to wear a plain veshti without a silk angavastram?

Yes, it depends entirely on family tradition and personal preference — there's no strict rule, and many grooms choose simpler options for smaller or more casual wedding functions.
