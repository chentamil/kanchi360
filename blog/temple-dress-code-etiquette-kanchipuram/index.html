Temple Dress Code and Etiquette in Kanchipuram: What Visitors Should Know

Visiting temples in a town like Kanchipuram comes with a few dress and behaviour expectations that aren't always obvious to first-time visitors, especially foreign travellers. Here's what to know before you go.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 16 Jan 2026 · Updated 16 Jan 2026 · 6 min read

General Dress Code

Most temples in Kanchipuram expect modest dress covering shoulders and knees for both men and women. Sleeveless tops, shorts, and short skirts are generally discouraged or may not be permitted at all in the more actively worshipped temples. For women, a saree, salwar kameez, or long skirt/dress is a safe choice; for men, trousers or a veshti/dhoti paired with a shirt work well. Some temples specifically require men to remove shirts before entering the innermost sanctum areas, following older temple traditions, though this varies by temple.

Warning: Rules can vary between temples and are sometimes enforced more strictly during specific rituals or festival days, so it's worth dressing conservatively by default rather than assuming casual wear will be fine everywhere.

Footwear

Footwear must be removed before entering temple premises, typically at a designated area near the entrance, often with a small fee or a token system for safekeeping. Wearing easily removable footwear like sandals rather than lace-up shoes makes this far more convenient, especially if you're visiting multiple temples in one day.

General Behaviour Inside Temples

  • Photography may be restricted in certain inner areas — look for signage or ask before photographing deities directly
  • Keep voices low, especially near active worship or ritual areas
  • Some inner sanctum areas may be restricted to Hindu worshippers only at certain temples — this varies, so check locally rather than assuming access
  • Walking barefoot on stone floors can get very hot in direct sun during the day — early morning or evening visits are more comfortable

For Foreign and First-Time Visitors

If you're unfamiliar with temple customs, it's completely normal to observe what other visitors are doing or to politely ask temple staff or a local guide about specific rules at that particular temple — customs can differ meaningfully between temples even within the same town, and asking is far better than guessing.

Did You Know: The practice of removing footwear before entering temple premises is rooted in the idea of maintaining the sanctity of consecrated ground, a custom shared broadly across Hindu temple traditions in India.
Can non-Hindu tourists visit temples in Kanchipuram?

Many temples do welcome visitors of all backgrounds into the outer courtyards and halls, though access to the innermost sanctum can be restricted at some temples. It's best to check locally at each specific temple rather than assuming the same rule applies everywhere.

Is there an entry fee for temples in Kanchipuram?

Major temples are generally free to enter, though some may have optional fees for special darshan queues, camera permits, or specific inner areas. Basic entry to the main temple halls is typically free.
