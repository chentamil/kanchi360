Original vs Duplicate: How to Identify a Genuine Kanchipuram Saree Using Silk Mark and GI Tag

Two official markers can help protect you from duplicates: the Silk Mark label and the Geographical Indication (GI) tag for Kanchipuram silk. Here's what each one actually certifies, and what it doesn't.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 6 Jan 2026 · Updated 6 Jan 2026 · 8 min read

Two Different Certifications, Two Different Purposes

Buyers often assume Silk Mark and the GI tag are the same thing. They aren't, and understanding the difference helps you know exactly what protection you're getting when you see either one on a saree.

What Silk Mark Certifies

Silk Mark is a quality assurance label issued under the Silk Mark Organisation of India, a body promoted by the Central Silk Board. A Silk Mark tag certifies that the fabric is made of natural silk fibre, as opposed to art silk, polyester or a silk-synthetic blend being sold as pure silk. It does not, by itself, certify that the saree was woven specifically in Kanchipuram — a Silk Mark tag can appear on silk products from anywhere in India.

Did You Know: Silk Mark tags carry a unique authentication number that can be checked to confirm the tag itself is genuine, since counterfeit tags do unfortunately circulate.

What the GI Tag Certifies

The Geographical Indication (GI) tag is a separate government registration that protects the name 'Kanchipuram Silk Saree' itself, similar to how Champagne can only legally be called Champagne if it comes from that region of France. Kanchipuram Silk was granted GI status, meaning that only sarees woven by registered weavers in the designated Kanchipuram region using the traditional korvai technique and specified silk/zari standards can legally carry the 'Kanchipuram Silk' name for commercial sale. A GI-tagged saree usually comes with an authorised user certificate or tag from the weavers' association.

  • Silk Mark = the fibre is genuine silk (fabric quality claim)
  • GI Tag = the saree was genuinely woven in the Kanchipuram region under the traditional method (origin claim)
  • A saree can carry both, either, or neither — ask specifically which certification is being offered

Practical Ways to Spot a Duplicate

Beyond the official tags, a few hands-on checks help. Genuine mulberry silk has a slightly textured, warm handfeel, unlike the cool, slippery texture of polyester 'art silk' imitations. The korvai border join should be tight and even, without visible machine-zigzag stitching at the seam — an authentic korvai join is woven, not stitched after the fact. Pure zari has a duller, warmer gold-silver tone compared to the bright, almost plastic shine of synthetic metallic thread.

Warning: A shop simply stating 'this is a Kanchipuram saree' with no visible tag, certificate, or willingness to explain the weave should raise a flag, especially for a high-value purchase.

Questions to Ask Before Buying

  • Does this saree carry a Silk Mark tag, a GI authorised-user tag, or both?
  • Is the zari pure (real silver-gilt) or tested (alloy)?
  • Can I see the korvai join up close?
  • Is there a printed bill listing the material composition?
Does every genuine Kanchipuram saree have a GI tag on it?

Not always — many genuine handloom sarees from local weavers are sold without a physical GI tag attached, especially through smaller family-run shops, even though they were woven correctly in the region. The GI tag is most consistently seen on sarees sold through larger showrooms and official weaver cooperative outlets.

Can machine-made sarees carry a Silk Mark tag?

Silk Mark certifies fibre content, not the weaving method, so a machine-woven silk fabric can technically carry Silk Mark if the fibre itself is genuine silk. It does not by itself guarantee handloom weaving or Kanchipuram origin — that's precisely why the GI tag exists as a separate, additional layer of assurance.

Where can I verify a Silk Mark tag number?

The Silk Mark Organisation of India provides verification through their official channels using the unique number printed on the tag — ask the shop for this number and keep your bill so you can verify it after purchase.
