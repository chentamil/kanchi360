Best Photography Spots in Kanchipuram

Kanchipuram's temple architecture, weaving scenes and street life offer plenty for photographers. Here's where to point your camera, and a few etiquette notes to keep in mind.

By Kanchi360 Editorial · Published 31 Jan 2026 · Updated 31 Jan 2026 · 6 min read

Temple Gopurams and Pillared Halls

The towering gopurams (temple gateway towers) and intricately carved pillared halls at temples like Ekambareswarar and Varadharaja Perumal are natural photography highlights, particularly in the soft light of early morning or late afternoon rather than harsh midday sun.

Kailasanathar Temple's Stone Carvings

Being one of the oldest and quieter temples in town, Kailasanathar offers a more relaxed setting for photographing detailed Pallava-era stone carving work without the crowds found at more actively worshipped temples.

Quick Tip: Golden hour — shortly after sunrise or before sunset — gives temple stone architecture a warmth that flat midday light simply doesn't.

Silk Weaving Scenes

If you arrange a visit to a working handloom (through a cooperative or guide), the weaving process itself — coloured threads, zari work in progress, the loom mechanics — makes for genuinely unique photographs you won't get anywhere else, and a nice contrast to temple architecture shots.

Market Streets and Everyday Life

The market streets around the silk shopping area and near the bus stand offer candid, colourful street photography opportunities — saree displays, local vendors, and everyday town life.

Silk market streets offer some of Kanchipuram's most colourful candid photography

Photography Etiquette

  • Always check for signage or ask before photographing inner sanctum areas of temples, since restrictions vary by site
  • Ask before photographing individuals directly, especially in more personal settings like a weaver's home workshop
  • Be mindful during active worship or ritual moments rather than treating everything as a photo opportunity
  • Early morning generally offers both better light and fewer crowds for temple photography
Are drones allowed for photography around Kanchipuram's temples?

Drone use around religious sites and certain public areas in India is often restricted or requires permission — check current local regulations before planning any drone photography, rather than assuming it's allowed.

Is a professional camera needed, or is a phone camera good enough?

Modern phone cameras handle most casual photography needs well, especially in good light. A dedicated camera helps mainly for low-light interior temple shots or if you want more control over depth of field for detailed carving close-ups.
